# M4-12 — Language-aware distribution of files to workers

Decision D025.

## Before

A batch's files went to whichever worker was free. Every worker therefore loaded the grammar of every language it met:
- the generated six-language bench on 8 workers made 40 grammar loads;
- three copies of MapX's sources made 30 loads (several languages and static files).

## Delivered (`src/core/worker-pool.ts`)

- **`AffinityScheduler`** plans each batch:
  - **Segment layout:** the batch's languages are laid end to end, the heaviest first and static files last, and cut into one equal segment per lane (a lane is one worker). Each file goes to the segment holding its midpoint.
  - **Effects:** a big language spans several lanes, and small languages share the last lane. A lane holds only the languages crossing its segment, so grammar loads are at most lanes + languages − 1, and the lanes' work is equal.
  - **Warm pool:** a segment goes to a lane whose worker already holds its main language. `PoolWorker.languages` records the languages each worker has parsed across batches.
- **`next(lane)`** decides what a lane parses:
  1. files a crashed worker left (any lane);
  2. the lane's own queue;
  3. another lane's files of a language this lane holds, or static files;
  4. otherwise, from the largest remaining queue, but only when at least the "penalty" is left there. The penalty is one grammar load's worth (110 KB of real code, about 100 ms), at most a quarter of a lane's share. Below that the lane ends, and the owner lane finishes.
- **`LaneQueue`:** each lane's files grouped by language in plan order, with head pointers and running weights. Taking from the front, taking a language's last file, and reading a lane's remaining weight are all cheap. A first version used arrays with `shift()` and summed every queue on each call: 400,000 files took 6,184 ms to schedule, against 119 ms now.
- **Weights:** bytes, with static-tier files at a twentieth, as in M4-13's sizing.
- **Docs:** `docs/configuration.md` "Parallel parsing" and the CHANGELOG.

## Measurements (16 cores; the index is identical to `--no-parallel` in every row)

| Bench | Before (M4-13, `ee20492` worktree) | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generated, 3,000 files, 6 languages, `MAPX_PARSE_WORKERS=8` | 3,559 ms, 992 MB, 40 grammar loads | 3,186 ms, 927 MB, 20 grammar loads |
| MapX sources ×3 (829 files), auto (8 workers) | 6,091 ms, 1,355 MB, 30 grammar loads | 5,878 ms, 1,350 MB, 12 grammar loads |

Criterion 3, wall time no worse than 10% above M4-13: it is 10% and 3.5% faster.
- **Generated bench:** loads stop at 20, not the plan's 13, because the six languages cost different amounts per byte. Lanes finish at different times, and the steals in step 4 then load some grammars.
- **Memory:** almost unchanged, because a grammar is small next to a worker's V8 isolate and parser modules.

## Independent review (fresh agent)

**Correctness: no P0 or P1.** A harness counting how often each file is handed out across `next()` and `drain()` saw every file exactly once in all of these cases:
- the 300/150/6/6/6 mix;
- a single language, and all-static batches;
- more lanes than files, an empty batch, and one 1 MB file;
- `requeue` and `resetLane` mid-batch.

**No deadlock.** A lane ends only when its own queue is empty. Crashed files go to the retry queue, and with restarts exhausted, the owner hands its files back.

**Findings:**
- **P2, already fixed when reported:** quadratic scheduling. The review ran on the array version; the `LaneQueue` rewrite (400,000 files in 119 ms) was made while it ran.
- **P2, fixed:** the warm pool was matched greedily, and the batch took the first `wanted` live workers whatever they held. For example, lanes holding {php, ts} and {php} given php and ts took a needless load, and a {go} and {ruby} pool given php and go put go on the ruby lane. Fixed in two parts:
  - the batch takes the live workers holding most of its weight;
  - segments are matched to lanes by overlap weight, largest first, with ties to the lane holding fewer languages.
  Both of the reviewer's examples are now tests.
- **P2, fixed:** `PoolWorker.languages` recorded `vue` and `svelte`, but those workers load the TypeScript grammar (checked with `MAPX_WORKER_STATS`). Vue and Svelte now map to the `typescript` key.
- **Tests, fixed:** 5 of the reviewer's 11 mutants survived. Each now has a test, and each fails it:

  | Mutant | Test that now catches it |
  | --- | --- |
  | No steal from the largest queue | Three languages and a fast lane: the lane must take another language, and the makespan stays under 80 ticks |
  | `resetLane` a no-op | A replaced lane must not steal below the penalty |
  | No `pw.languages` tracking | Real pool: recorded languages must equal loaded grammars, and a later Python batch adds no Python load |
  | Retry served after the own queue | Retry must come first |
  | `drain()` without retry | `drain()` must include retries |

  A sixth mutant, taking the first `wanted` workers, also fails the real-pool test. That test passed 3 of 3 repeated runs.
- **Criterion 3, wall time:** the reviewer's timings were inconclusive. The load average was 9–57 from other processes, and one-thread time swung from 3 s to 32 s.
  - **Their runs:** generated 3,081/3,401/4,030 ms against base 3,466/3,530/3,456, with 13–15 grammar loads against 40.
  - **My paired runs above** show 10% and 3.5% gains. Grammar loads fell in every run.
  - The load average was still about 29 at close. A quiet-machine rerun is left to the M4-14 measurements.
